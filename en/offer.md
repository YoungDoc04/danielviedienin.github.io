    <li>Ability to talk with clinicians.</li>
    <li>Identifying a doctor's hidden needs through observation and listening.</li>

    <li>Collecting and interpreting feedback as a basis for improvements.</li>

  <h3>Why I fit</h3>
  <p>I understand the importance of listening and hearing, because I have worked with people on their scariest day. I can quickly find common ground, explain complex things in simple language, train, and build trust. The client won't be afraid to call me with questions. I can help them not just start using the product, but believe in it and recommend it to others.</p>
</section>

<section class="role-block">

  <h3>My advantages</h3>
  <ul>

    <li>Organizing the implementation process: from the first tests to a full launch.</li>
    <li>Training staff and translating doctors' needs into the language of development.</li>

I will collect feedback and translate it into the developer's language. This is not just "implementation"; it is a comprehensive process to make the product actually work in live conditions.</p>
</section>

<section class="role-block">
  <h2>Sales Engineer / Solutions Consultant</h2>
  <p>A technical expert who supports sales in complex deals: shows how the product works – conducts demonstrations, adapting them to the client's goals, participates in negotiations, answers technical questions, helps the client see the solution, and connects them with the development team. Helps sales managers form proposals (RFPs, tenders, commercial proposals) from the technical side. Prepares and maintains technical materials.</p>
  <h3>My advantages</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Establishing contact with any skeptic. Asking clarifying questions, then letting them speak – and the skeptic has already become a participant in the dialogue.</li>
    <li>Explaining complex things in the client's language – using analogies from their world, checking understanding; not moving on until the person has grasped the essence.</li>
    <li>Honestly admitting when something is unknown, then actually coming back with a clear answer. This builds trust more strongly than trying to wriggle out of it.</li>
    <li>Identifying real needs. Listening not to words, but to what lies behind them: what a person repeats, what they avoid, where their tone changes. The real request is often hidden behind reluctance – a consequence of fear or fatigue.</li>
    <li>Confidently handling objections and aggression without taking it personally. Listening, letting them speak, smoothly shifting to the plane of "what doesn't suit you – let's break it down."</li>
    <li>Translating doctors' needs into developers' language, and vice versa. For example: a doctor isn't receiving data because your system doesn't transmit data to their system – into "make an API."</li>
    <li>Quickly learning a new product. Show me: first, why it is needed and who the target user is; then, the 20% of features that give 80% of the result; after that, practice and everything else.</li>
    <li>Understanding when the client is ready – they stop asking "how does this work" and start suggesting "could we also do it like this." This is a signal: trust is there, and not losing it is a small matter when you know how to listen and react in time.</li>
  </ul>
  <h3>Why I fit</h3>
  <p>Even though I am not a programmer in the classical sense and cannot solve a code problem on my own, once I understand how your system works from the inside and begin talking with both sides, I will organize the process so that the client quickly engages with the essence and code issues are quickly resolved by specialists. And I can always explain how to squeeze the maximum out of the product, even if it is still raw, and how further work will be improved by testing, especially when it is conducted by a qualified specialist.</p>
</section>

  <h3>My advantages</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Listening sincerely.</li>
    <li>Ability to be a neutral party in a conflict.</li>
    <li>Explaining complex things in simple words.</li>
    <li>Respecting everyone's role and experience.</li>
    <li>Emotional stability.</li>
    <li>Holding an audience's attention and conveying a thought clearly.</li>
    <li>Seeing exactly where communication breaks down.</li>
    <li>Building the entire training process.</li>
    <li>Measuring the result: whether there are fewer conflicts, whether efficiency has grown.</li>
  </ul>
  <h3>Why I fit</h3>
  <p>Behind aggression and anger there is often hurt. I can remain neutral, explain the importance of teamwork, remind people about self-reflection and the importance of not devaluing a person's experience. The main principle is to listen and not give random advice, because the answer is already hidden in the interlocutor's words.</p>
