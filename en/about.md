<section class="about-block flame">

  <p class="lead">Over 3+ years – more than 4,000 emergency responses.</p>
  <p>Hundreds of 24-hour shifts, thousands of patients, countless scenarios that aren't taught in textbooks. This isn't just a "job". I have to make decisions where the cost of a mistake can be someone's life.</p>
  <p class="lead">In critical situations, there is no time for panic. Only maintaining composure and a clear sequence of actions.</p>
</section>

<section class="about-block flame">
  <h2>PSYCHOLOGY AND COMMUNICATION</h2>
  <p class="lead">I know how to speak so that people actually listen.</p>
  <p>Over the years, I've worked with: veterans with PTSD, mothers who lost their children, alcoholics in delirium tremens, drug addicts in withdrawal. All of them – people in distress and in panic.</p>
  <p>Finding mutual understanding isn't magic – it's the result of thousands of attempts to understand a person in an emergency, through respect for personal boundaries and awareness of their state, "here and now."</p>
  <p class="lead">This isn't just a "communication skill." It's the ability to keep a clear head when everything around you is chaos, and not to create new, unnecessary resentment.</p>
</section>

<section class="about-block flame">
  <h2>SALES AND NEGOTIATIONS</h2>
  <p class="lead">I worked in B2B sales – veterinary pharmaceuticals.</p>
  <p>Back then, nobody taught me how to sell – I had to find ways to negotiate with people who were a level above me, in situations where others saw a dead end. I studied the material, traveled to clinics, built relationships with veterinarians, and delivered consistent results.</p>
  <p>Over time, the company's focus shifted – they started demanding reports that had nothing to do with sales. When random metrics became the priority, I refused to work just "for show" – and I left on my own.</p>
  <p class="lead">That experience reinforced my stance: I choose to work where real results are valued, not paper illusions.</p>
</section>

<section class="about-block flame">

  <p>The rich fear losing power. The poor fear losing themselves. These are different languages, but I speak both. Economics isn't numbers or complex formulas. Above all, it's people – who want, doubt, and fear. They buy emotions, even when they don't need the product.</p>
  <p class="lead">I don't manipulate this. I use it to create ideas that work for everyone.</p>
</section>

<section class="about-block steel">
  <h2>WHY HEALTHTECH?</h2>
  <p class="lead">In short: the future lies in integrating AI into daily medical practice.</p>
  <p>I've worked where mistakes aren't allowed. And I realized: the key isn't the speed of your hands, but instantaneous correct decisions.</p>
  <p>Experience will teach this over time, but what always works is an algorithm. If an algorithm can help a doctor work better and faster – I want to promote that algorithm.</p>
  <p>This is where statistics come in: millions of people in need, hundreds of thousands of emergencies, thousands of mass incidents. Daily. Everywhere. Patients are all different: losing consciousness, aggressive, frightened, demanding. You have to quickly read a person, differentiate, choose a tactic in seconds, and speak so that each one listens and trusts. In working with lives, there's no room for fuss – only systematicity.</p>
  <p class="lead">The conclusion suggests itself: technology must be faster than chaos.</p>
</section>
