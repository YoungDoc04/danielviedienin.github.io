I'm a paramedic working shifts for the Regional Center for Emergency
Medical Care and Disaster Medicine in Dnipropetrovsk region. Over the last
3 years and 2 months I've responded to more than a thousand emergency
calls — cardiac events, trauma, everything an ambulance crew is called out
for on a given night.

## Where it started

Before medicine, I worked in B2B sales in veterinary pharmaceuticals —
building relationships with clinics, understanding a technical product
well enough to sell it, and learning how to talk to people who are under
pressure and short on time. A lot of that transferred directly into
prehospital care: reading a room fast, explaining something clearly in
ten seconds, staying calm when someone else can't.

## Medicine, and where AI fits in

I'm currently a medical student, studying alongside my shifts. On the
road, I've started integrating ChatGPT into how I work: running through
differentials faster when a case doesn't fit a clean pattern, and using it
to draft and complete the documentation every call requires, so less of a
shift gets lost to paperwork. It's a small habit, but it's changed how
much I can actually think about the patient in front of me instead of the
form behind them.

## Where I'm headed

That habit is what's pulling me toward HealthTech. I want to keep working
close to patients while building the tools that make frontline medicine
faster and less error-prone — software written by someone who has
actually stood in the back of an ambulance, not just studied it.

If you're building in HealthTech, hiring, or just want to compare notes
on AI in emergency medicine, my [contact page](/contact/) has the details.
